Project Overview

The business problem, objectives, and analytical solution.

Business Problem

A global airline group needed a unified way to monitor operational reliability, revenue efficiency, cost structure, and customer experience across ten airline identities. Data was scattered across flight operations, disruptions, customer feedback, bookings, and cost ledgers — with no single source of truth for executive decision-making.

Objectives

  • Build a 16-table star-schema semantic model from curated flight, booking, cost, disruption, and feedback data.
  • Define 54 governed DAX measures across flight operations, revenue, costs, and customer experience.
  • Deliver five executive Power BI report pages with 120 visuals.
  • Validate everything with 609 automated tests (595 core + 14 canonicalization) and 34/34 manifest hash verification.
  • Record and enforce governance decisions (OD-04g, GOV-D-04 through GOV-D-08, D-02, CC-01, OD-01A, OD-01B) and gate approvals (G3-RV-01, schema freeze, G3, G4) — all closed 08 November 2026.

Solution

A reproducible Python data pipeline generates and validates synthetic datasets, Power Query M loads them into the Power BI semantic model, DAX measures compute KPIs, and five report pages deliver executive insight — all under a strict "synthetic simulation" disclosure.

Scope

10 Real Airline Identities
12 months Reporting Period (2025-10-01 to 2026-09-30)

Business Insights & Recommendations

Verified observations and recommended actions drawn from the synthetic AEROVISION 360 datasets.

Synthetic data disclaimer: All observations below are derived from synthetic simulation data. They demonstrate analytical methodology and business storytelling — they do not represent actual airline performance.

Page 01

Executive Oversight — Network Health

Key observation: Network reliability varies by airline, with on-time departure (OP01) and cancellation rate (FL04) showing the widest spread across the ten identities.

Supporting KPIs: FL01 Flights Operated · FL02 Revenue Passengers · OP01 On-Time Departure · FL04 Cancellation Rate · DS01 Total Disruptions

Business implication: A small subset of airlines likely drives most network disruption volume, suggesting targeted intervention rather than network-wide changes.

Recommended action: Prioritise the bottom-quartile airlines for operational review; deploy disruption-category root-cause analysis (Page 02) before scheduling changes.

Page 02

Operations — Disruption Concentration

Key observation: Disruption events (DS01) are concentrated in specific disruption types and route regions, with controllable disruptions (DS03) representing a reducible portion.

Supporting KPIs: OP01 · OP03 Avg Arrival Delay · OP04 Completion · DS03 Controllable Disruptions · FL04 Cancellation Rate

Business implication: Controllable disruptions are within airline operational influence and offer the highest ROI for process improvement.

Recommended action: Focus recovery initiatives on controllable disruption categories; use completion rate trends to set quarterly reliability targets per airline.

Page 03

Revenue — Channel & Fare Mix

Key observation: Revenue concentration by booking channel and fare class reveals where yield management effort yields the greatest return.

Supporting KPIs: RV_TotalOperatingRevenue · RV_NetPassengerRevenue · RV_NetAncillaryRevenue · Passenger Yield · Total RASK

Business implication: Ancillary revenue (RV_NetAncillaryRevenue) is a high-margin lever; channel mix determines distribution cost efficiency.

Recommended action: Shift mix toward direct channels and ancillary-eligible fare classes; monitor RASK by route to identify underpriced segments.

Page 04

Cost — Efficiency by Category

Key observation: Operating costs concentrate in fuel, crew, airport charges, and maintenance; cost per flight operation (CX) varies meaningfully by airline and route.

Supporting KPIs: CX_TotalOperatingCosts · CX_CostPerFlightOperation · CX_CostPerRevenuePassenger · NC_NetOperatingContribution · Revenue-to-Cost Ratio

Business implication: The revenue-to-cost ratio (0.0465) indicates the synthetic cost model absorbs full category costs per flight while revenue is coupon-limited — a structural feature to document for stakeholders.

Recommended action: Benchmark cost-per-flight by airline and route; identify outliers for crew and fuel efficiency programmes.

Page 05

Customer — Satisfaction & Recovery

Key observation: Satisfaction (CF02) and complaint rate (CF03) vary by airline and feedback channel; service recovery rate (CF11) indicates where complaints are successfully resolved.

Supporting KPIs: CF01 Total Feedback · CF02 Avg Satisfaction · CF03 Complaint % · CF11 Service Recovery · CF12 Resolution Rate

Business implication: Channels with high complaint volume and low recovery rates are priority targets for service-process redesign.

Recommended action: Route high-volume complaint categories to dedicated recovery teams; track resolution rate as a weekly service KPI.

Methodology: Each observation is traceable to a specific KPI and report page in the AEROVISION 360 Power BI model. All figures are synthetic; recommendations illustrate the analytical decision-making pattern this portfolio enables.
