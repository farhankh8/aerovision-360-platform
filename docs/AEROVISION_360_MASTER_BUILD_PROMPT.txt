Airline identity and reference-version rules:
- `AirlineId` is the durable business identity. `AirlineKey` is the permanent warehouse surrogate used by facts and Power BI relationships. They are not interchangeable.
- IATA and ICAO codes are mutable, version-qualified attributes, not keys. `AirlineName` is the commercial brand. `LegalName` is optional and reference/provenance-only.
- `HomeCountry` is the principal headquarters or domicile country. `AirlineGroup` is the optional ultimate parent company or group brand; it is not inferred.
- `BusinessModelClass` is an optional analytical classification, not a source-verified identity attribute. `OperatingRegion` is not an airline identity attribute; derive analytical region from route and airport geography.
- Use `IsActive` and `StatusEffectiveDate` for the minimal lifecycle. Do not add `LifecycleStatus` or `MergedIntoAirlineId` in the baseline.
- `DimAirline` is Type 1. Immutable reference snapshots preserve build reproducibility but not as-was historical identity labels. Type 2 is deferred until a future requirement demands it.
- The reference manifest is the authoritative approval record. `DimAirline.ReferenceVersion` is technical lineage only. Every generation and refresh pins exactly one approved `ReferenceVersion`; mixed versions are prohibited.
- The airline key map is controlled and immutable after OD-01B approval. The same `AirlineId` must always resolve to the same `AirlineKey`, regardless of CSV row order, rebuild, or database identity sequence. Facts are never re-keyed.
- Reference-version approval uses `G3-RV-01`, not an OD-series decision. OD-23 is rejected and is not created. T-78 verifies manifest, version, refresh, and key integrity.
- Every populated identity attribute requires attribute-level provenance with a real source, retrieval date, verification date, verification status, and durable evidence. Unsupported optional attributes remain null; conflicting or unsupported values are quarantined.

8. KPI DICTIONARY AND CALCULATION CORRECTNESS
Before writing DAX, document each critical KPI's definition, formula, unit, numerator, denominator, grain, aggregation, exclusions, filter behavior, zero-denominator behavior, and validation example.

Core airline economics:
- ASK = available seats × flight distance.
- RPK = eligible revenue passengers carried × flight distance.
- Passenger load factor = RPK ÷ ASK.
- Passenger yield = passenger revenue ÷ RPK.
- Passenger RASK = passenger revenue ÷ ASK.
- Total RASK = defined operating revenue ÷ ASK.
- CASK = defined operating cost ÷ ASK.
- Unit contribution = defined revenue per ASK − defined cost per ASK.
- Average passenger revenue and ancillary revenue per eligible passenger.
- Revenue and cost growth against comparable prior periods.

Operational KPIs:
- On-time departure and arrival percentages.
- Average departure and arrival delay.
- Delay minutes per operated flight.
- Cancellation rate and completion factor.
- Scheduled versus operated flights.
- Aircraft utilization.
- Disruption rate and reactionary-delay share.

Commercial/customer KPIs:
- Booking lead time and booking-channel mix.
- Fare-class performance.
- Refund rate and value.
- Ancillary revenue share.
- Satisfaction, complaints, loyalty segments, repeat bookings where supported, and service recovery.

Management KPIs:
- Airline and route rankings.
- Actual versus target variance only when approved target data is available; otherwise report target variance as unavailable.
- Route contribution and operational exceptions against explicitly approved thresholds, where defined.
- Operational hotspots and scenario sensitivity.

Document treatment of taxes, fees, refunds, cancellations, non-revenue passengers, codeshares, and disrupted flights where relevant. Define the on-time threshold explicitly. Do not silently exclude canceled flights from relevant denominators. Reactionary delays must be based on explicit event/cause data, not guessed from the existence of a delay. Independently reconcile critical DAX measures against control calculations.
